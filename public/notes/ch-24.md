# Chapter 24: Interfacing Sensors with Arduino

## Core Concept
Sensors connect the physical world to an Arduino. Depending on their electrical characteristics, sensors interface through raw analog voltages, digital logic levels, or specialized serial communication buses (like One-Wire, I2C, or SPI).

---

## 1. Interfacing Analog Sensors (Example: LDR & Potentiometers)
* **Voltage Divider Circuit:**
  * Microcontrollers cannot read variable resistance directly; they can only measure voltage.
  * A resistive sensor (such as a Light Dependent Resistor or thermistor) is placed in series with a fixed reference resistor ($R_{ref}$) between 5V and GND.
  * The voltage at the midpoint between the two resistors is routed to an analog input pin (A0 to A5).
* **ADC Conversion Formula:**
  $$\text{ADC Output} = \frac{V_{in}}{5.0} \times 1023$$
* Software maps raw ADC integers (0 to 1023) to meaningful engineering units using formulas or the built-in `map()` utility.

---

## 2. Interfacing Digital Environmental Sensors (Example: DHT11 / DHT22)
* **DHT Sensors:** Combined digital sensors that measure ambient temperature and relative humidity.
  * **DHT11:** Lower cost, temperature range 0 to 50 degrees Celsius (+/- 2 degrees error), humidity range 20% to 90% RH (+/- 5% error).
  * **DHT22 (AM2302):** Higher accuracy, temperature range -40 to 80 degrees Celsius (+/- 0.5 degrees error), humidity range 0% to 100% RH.
* **Single-Wire Digital Protocol:** Uses a proprietary single-bus bidirectional line with a pull-up resistor.
* **Data Transmission Packet:** Transmits 40 bits of serial data:
  * 16 bits: Humidity data (integer and decimal parts).
  * 16 bits: Temperature data (integer and decimal parts).
  * 8 bits: Checksum (parity check verifying data integrity).

---

## 3. Ultrasonic Distance Sensor Interfacing (HC-SR04)
* **Hardware Pins:** VCC (5V), GND, Trigger (digital output), Echo (digital input).
* **Operation Workflow:**
  1. Microcontroller sends a 10-microsecond `HIGH` pulse to the **Trigger** pin.
  2. The module emits an 8-cycle ultrasonic burst at 40 kHz.
  3. The **Echo** pin goes `HIGH` for the exact duration it takes for the sound wave to travel out, reflect off an obstacle, and return.
  4. The microcontroller measures this pulse width using `pulseIn(echoPin, HIGH)`.
  5. Distance is computed using the speed of sound in air (approximately 0.034 cm/microsecond):
     $$\text{Distance (cm)} = \frac{\text{Time (microseconds)} \times 0.034}{2}$$

---

## Key Exam Takeaways
* **Voltage Divider Rationale:** Converts variable resistance changes into measurable analog voltages.
* **DHT Packet Size:** Exactly 40 bits total, including an 8-bit checksum.
* **DHT22 vs DHT11:** DHT22 provides wider operational range and higher measurement precision.
* **HC-SR04 Trigger Pulse:** Requires a 10-microsecond digital pulse to initiate acoustic ping.
* **Echo Division by 2:** Required because the measured time covers the round-trip distance (out and back).
