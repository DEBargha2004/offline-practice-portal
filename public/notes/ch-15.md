# Chapter 15: Sensor Networks — Part II: Node Behavior & Faults

## Core Concept
In distributed sensor networks, nodes are prone to hardware failure, environmental interference, and protocol non-compliance. Identifying and managing unexpected node behaviors is essential to preserving network lifetime, routing integrity, and data fidelity.

---

## 1. Classification of Node Behaviors

### Normal Nodes
* Operate strictly according to network protocols.
* Actively sense, transmit their own packets, and willingly forward multi-hop traffic for neighboring nodes.

### Misbehaving Nodes
Nodes that deviate from normal protocol execution, categorized by underlying cause:
1. **Selfish Nodes:**
   * Functionally intact nodes that intentionally drop transit packets or refuse to forward routing requests.
   * **Motivation:** Conserving their own finite battery reserves for their own personal sensing data rather than spending energy helping others.
2. **Malicious / Compromised Nodes:**
   * Nodes taken over by an adversary or running corrupted firmware.
   * Actively inject false sensor readings, launch selective forwarding attacks, alter packet headers, or jam channels.
3. **Faulty / Defective Nodes:**
   * Nodes experiencing hardware degradation (such as failing ADC circuitry, drained batteries, or damaged antenna connections).
   * Generates noisy or completely erratic telemetry.

---

## 2. Dumb Behavior in Sensor Networks
* **Definition:** A unique unintentional failure mode where a sensor node successfully samples its physical surroundings and generates valid packets, but **cannot transmit them** over the radio.
* **Root Causes:**
  * **Environmental Signal Attenuation:** Sudden dense foliage, rainfall, or physical obstacles blocking the radio path.
  * **Transmission Range Shrinkage:** As battery voltage drops below nominal thresholds, transceiver transmission power decreases, shrinking the communication radius so it can no longer reach any neighbor.
* **Consequences:** The node becomes an isolated data island, wasting energy sensing information that cannot be delivered.

---

## 3. Mitigation and Trust Frameworks
* **Watchdog Mechanisms:** Neighboring nodes listen in promiscuous mode to verify whether a forwarder node actually transmits a packet that was handed to it.
* **Reputation & Trust Systems:** Nodes track cooperation metrics for peers. Selfish nodes that consistently drop packets are penalized and excluded from trusted routing paths.
* **Dynamic Transmission Power Control:** Automatically raising power levels when radio range shrinkage is detected to re-establish connectivity.

---

## Key Exam Takeaways
* **Selfish vs. Malicious Nodes:** Selfish nodes drop packets solely to preserve their own battery; Malicious nodes deliberately disrupt or compromise the network.
* **Dumb Behavior Cause:** Transmission range shrinkage or environmental signal blockage preventing sensed data from being sent.
* **Detection Mechanism:** Neighbor promiscuous monitoring (watchdog monitoring) to track packet forwarding ratios.
* **Impact of Packet Dropping:** Degrades network coverage, breaks multi-hop paths, and forces expensive route rediscovery.
