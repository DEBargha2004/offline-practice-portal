# Chapter 56: Data Handling & Analytics — Part II: Statistical Models & Methods

## Core Concept
Transforming raw sensor streams into actionable scientific insights requires systematic data analytics. This chapter explores quantitative vs. qualitative methods, dispersion statistics, correlation, regression models, and parametric hypothesis testing such as Analysis of Variance (ANOVA).

---

## 1. Qualitative vs. Quantitative Analytics
* **Qualitative Analysis:** Evaluates non-numerical, categorical, or narrative data (user survey transcripts, audio logs, behavioral notes) to identify underlying themes.
* **Quantitative Analysis:** Applies mathematical and statistical algorithms to numerical measurements (temperature, voltage, pressure, packet loss ratios) to test hypotheses and establish predictive models.

---

## 2. Descriptive Statistics & Data Dispersion
* **Measures of Central Tendency:** Mean (average value), Median (middle value in ordered list), Mode (most frequently occurring value).
* **Measures of Dispersion (Spread):**
  * **Range:** Difference between maximum and minimum observed readings.
  * **Variance:** The average of the squared deviations from the mean; measures how far readings spread out from the average.
  * **Standard Deviation:** Square root of the variance, expressed in the same physical units as the original sensor data.
* **Contingency Tables:** Matrix tables displaying frequency counts across two or more categorical variables, uncovering interdependencies.

---

## 3. Inferential Modeling: Correlation & Regression
* **Correlation Analysis:** Measures the strength and direction of a linear relationship between two continuous variables (quantified by Pearson's correlation coefficient r, ranging from -1.0 to +1.0):
  * Positive correlation: As temperature increases, power consumption rises.
  * Negative correlation: As battery voltage decreases, transmission error rate rises.
* **Regression Analysis:** Models the mathematical relationship between one dependent target variable (Y) and one or more independent predictor variables (X):
  *Simple Linear Regression:* `Y = beta_0 + beta_1 * X + epsilon`
  Enables prediction of future sensor states based on environmental variables.

---

## 4. Hypothesis Testing: ANOVA (Analysis of Variance)
* **ANOVA:** A parametric statistical test used to determine whether there are statistically significant differences between the means of three or more independent experimental groups.
* **Application in IoT:** Comparing battery consumption across three competing routing protocols (e.g., RPL vs. AODV vs. LOADng) to determine if observed performance differences are statistically significant or due to random chance.

---

## Key Exam Takeaways
* **ANOVA Purpose:** Compares the means of three or more experimental groups to test for statistically significant differences.
* **Pearson Correlation Range:** Varies between -1.0 (perfect negative) and +1.0 (perfect positive).
* **Regression Purpose:** Predicts the value of a dependent variable from known independent predictor variables.
* **Standard Deviation:** Quantifies dispersion in the same units as the original sensor measurements.
