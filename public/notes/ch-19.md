# Chapter 19: UAV Networks & Flying Ad-Hoc Networks (FANET)

## Core Concept
Unmanned Aerial Vehicle (UAV) networks, or Flying Ad-Hoc Networks (FANETs), are self-configuring aerial networks formed by drones, quadcopters, and autonomous aircraft. They extend traditional Mobile Ad-Hoc Networks (MANETs) into three-dimensional space, providing aerial reconnaissance, disaster relief communications, and dynamic data collection.

---

## 1. Characteristics of FANETs
* **Three-Dimensional Mobility:** Nodes navigate freely along latitude, longitude, and altitude axes.
* **Very High Speed:** Aerial platforms travel at high velocities, causing rapid topology changes and frequent link breaks.
* **Predominantly Line-of-Sight (LoS):** Air-to-air and air-to-ground links enjoy clear line-of-sight with minimal ground obstruction, but are subject to antenna orientation shifts and wind turbulence.
* **Severe Energy Constraints:** High propulsion power demands (lifting and hovering) limit typical flight missions to tens of minutes unless tethered.

---

## 2. Network Topologies in UAV Networks
* **Star Topology:** All UAVs communicate directly with a single central ground control station (GCS). Simple, but limited by transmission distance and vulnerable to a single point of failure.
* **Mesh Topology:** UAVs establish peer-to-peer radio links with neighboring drones in the swarm. Provides multi-hop forwarding, self-healing communication, and resilience against individual drone crashes.
* **Multi-Layered / Multi-Plane Architecture:**
  * **Inter-Plane Communication:** UAVs at different flight altitudes (e.g., high-altitude surveillance gliders communicating with low-altitude tactical quadcopters) coordinate operations across hierarchical planes.

---

## Comparison: MANET vs. VANET vs. FANET
| Dimension | MANET (Mobile) | VANET (Vehicular) | FANET (Flying / UAV) |
| :--- | :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **Mobility Dimension** | 2D surface | 2D road infrastructure | Full 3D airspace |
| **Node Speed** | Low (pedestrians) | Moderate to High | High to Very High |
| **Topology Change Rate** | Slow to Moderate | Regular / constrained by roads | Extremely rapid and frequent |
| **Energy Source** | Small batteries | Vehicle alternator/battery | Flight battery/fuel (heavy drain) |
| **Line-of-Sight (LoS)** | Often blocked by buildings | Partially blocked by terrain | High probability of clear LoS |

---

## Key Exam Takeaways
* **FANET Definition:** Flying Ad-Hoc Network extending MANET principles into 3D aerial swarms.
* **Primary Design Hurdle:** High-velocity 3D mobility causing frequent link disconnects.
* **Energy Bottleneck:** High mechanical power required for aerodynamic lift and hovering.
* **Multi-Layer Benefit:** Coordinates high-altitude overview drones with low-altitude close-up inspection craft.
* **Mesh Topology Advantage:** Redundant multi-hop links maintain swarm coordination even if single drones fail.
