# Chapter 12: Connectivity Technologies — Part IV: Bluetooth & BLE

## Core Concept
Bluetooth (IEEE 802.15.1) is a short-range wireless standard designed for Personal Area Networks (PANs). While Classic Bluetooth targeted continuous audio streams and file transfers, Bluetooth Low Energy (BLE) was introduced for ultra-low power consumption in battery-powered IoT devices and wearables.

---

## 1. Bluetooth Core Architecture: Piconets and Scatternets
* **Piconet:** An ad-hoc network consisting of **1 Master** device and up to **7 active Slave** devices (with up to 255 parked slaves).
  * The Master controls the clock, synchronizes frequency hopping, and grants transmission permission.
  * Slaves communicate exclusively with the Master; direct slave-to-slave communication is prohibited.
* **Scatternet:** Formed when two or more independent piconets overlap. A single device can participate in multiple piconets by acting as a Slave in both, or as a Master in one and a Slave in another.

---

## 2. Radio & Physical Characteristics
* **Frequency Band:** 2.4 GHz ISM band.
* **Frequency Hopping:** Classic Bluetooth hops across 79 channels (1 MHz bandwidth each) at 1600 hops per second using Gaussian Frequency Shift Keying (GFSK).

---

## 3. Bluetooth Low Energy (BLE)
Introduced in Bluetooth 4.0, BLE is a completely redesigned protocol stack optimized for periodic, bursty sensor transmissions:
* **Channel Division:** Uses 40 channels (2 MHz bandwidth each):
  * **3 Advertising Channels (37, 38, 39):** Dedicated to device discovery, broadcasting, and connection setup, strategically placed to avoid common Wi-Fi channel overlaps.
  * **37 Data Channels:** Used for bidirectional data exchange once connected.
* **GATT (Generic Attribute Profile):** Client-server data exchange hierarchy:
  * **Profile:** Collection of related services (e.g., Heart Rate Profile).
  * **Service:** Logical group of data points (e.g., Heart Rate Measurement Service).
  * **Characteristic:** Individual data values with permissions and descriptors (e.g., Heart Rate Measurement value).

---

## Comparison: Classic Bluetooth vs. BLE
| Feature | Classic Bluetooth | Bluetooth Low Energy (BLE) |
| :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **Target Application** | Continuous audio streaming, bulk data | Bursty sensor telemetry, beacons |
| **Power Consumption** | Moderate (days to weeks) | Extremely low (months to years on coin cell) |
| **Channels** | 79 channels (1 MHz wide) | 40 channels (2 MHz wide) |
| **Duty Cycle** | Continuous / high | Very low (<1%) |
| **Advertising Channels** | Distributed across all 79 | 3 dedicated channels (37, 38, 39) |

---

## Key Exam Takeaways
* **Piconet Structure:** Exactly 1 Master and up to 7 active Slaves.
* **Scatternet Definition:** Multiple interconnected piconets bridged by shared nodes.
* **BLE Advertising Channels:** Channels 37, 38, and 39 avoid standard Wi-Fi interference.
* **GATT Hierarchy:** Profiles contain Services; Services contain Characteristics.
* **Power Profile:** BLE enables years of operation on tiny coin-cell batteries.
