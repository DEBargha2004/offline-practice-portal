# Chapter 25: Interfacing Actuators with Arduino

## Core Concept
Actuators convert Arduino electrical signals into mechanical motion or switching. Microcontroller I/O pins supply very low currents (maximum 20 to 40 mA at 5V), meaning inductive and high-power actuators (motors, solenoids, relays) require dedicated driver circuits, transistors, or external power supplies.

---

## 1. Servo Motors
A servo motor is an integrated closed-loop mechanical actuator that rotates to a specific angular position (typically 0 to 180 degrees) and holds that position.

### Internal Components
* DC motor, reduction gear train, position-sensing potentiometer, and onboard control circuit.

### Control Mechanism: Pulse Width Modulation (PWM)
* Servos expect a periodic control signal repeating every 20 milliseconds (50 Hz).
* The **duration of the positive pulse** dictates the target shaft angle:
  * Pulse of approximately 1.0 ms: Rotates to 0 degrees.
  * Pulse of approximately 1.5 ms: Centers the shaft at 90 degrees.
  * Pulse of approximately 2.0 ms: Rotates to 180 degrees.
* In Arduino, this is managed using the `<Servo.h>` library (`servo.attach(pin)` and `servo.write(angle)`).

---

## 2. Stepper Motors
* Brushless DC motors that divide a full 360-degree rotation into a series of equal discrete angular steps (e.g., 200 steps per revolution = 1.8 degrees per step).
* **Key Advantage:** Open-loop precise positioning—rotates to exact positions without needing an external encoder or feedback sensor.
* **Driving Circuits:** Controlled via H-bridges or dedicated stepper drivers (such as the ULN2003 or A4988) by energizing stator coils in specific step sequences (wave drive, full-step, half-step, or micro-stepping).

---

## 3. Relays and Flyback Protection
* **Relay Modules:** Used by microcontrollers to safely switch high-power circuits (such as 12V DC solenoids or 230V AC home appliances).
* **The Flyback Diode (Freewheeling Diode):**
  * When current to an inductive coil (relay, solenoid, or DC motor) is abruptly turned off, the collapsing magnetic field induces a massive reverse-voltage spike (back-EMF).
  * A diode placed in reverse-parallel across the coil safely dissipates this voltage spike, protecting the switching transistor and Arduino from destruction.

---

## Comparison: Servo Motor vs. Stepper Motor
| Feature | Servo Motor | Stepper Motor |
| :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **Motion Range** | Typically limited (0 to 180 degrees) | Continuous 360-degree rotation |
| **Feedback Mechanism** | Closed-loop (internal potentiometer) | Open-loop (counts commanded steps) |
| **Control Signal** | Pulse width duration (1 ms to 2 ms) | Step pulses and direction logic |
| **Holding Torque** | Holds commanded angular position | Holds step position as long as coils are energized |
| **Application** | Robot steering arms, RC rudders, valves | 3D printers, CNC routers, precision scanners |

---

## Key Exam Takeaways
* **Servo Control Period:** 20 ms refresh cycle (50 Hz frame).
* **Servo Pulse Widths:** Roughly 1.0 ms (0 deg), 1.5 ms (90 deg neutral), and 2.0 ms (180 deg).
* **Stepper Positioning Advantage:** Precise incremental rotation without requiring feedback sensors.
* **Flyback Diode Purpose:** Protects control electronics against high inductive back-EMF voltage spikes.
* **Arduino Max Pin Current:** Approximately 40 mA absolute maximum (external drivers mandatory for motors).
