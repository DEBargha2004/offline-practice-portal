# Chapter 50: Connected Vehicles — Part II: Intelligent Connected Vehicles (ICVs)

## Core Concept
Intelligent Connected Vehicles (ICVs) combine V2X wireless connectivity with advanced sensor suites (radar, LiDAR, cameras) and onboard Artificial Intelligence, transitioning beyond human driver warnings toward cooperative autonomous driving and collective crash prevention.

---

## 1. Core Hardware Infrastructure of ICVs
* **On-Board Unit (OBU):** Communication unit installed inside the vehicle. Interfaces with the in-vehicle CAN bus, runs safety applications, and controls 5.9 GHz transceivers.
* **Roadside Unit (RSU):** Fixed transceiver installed along highways, toll booths, and intersections. Broadcasts local road surface conditions, construction warnings, and routes data to cloud backends.

---

## 2. Safety Applications and Blind Spot Elimination
* **Non-Line-of-Sight (NLOS) Sensing:** Physical vehicle cameras and LiDAR cannot see through buildings, dense fog, or large shipping trucks. V2X radio waves penetrate obstructions:
  * **Intersection Collision Avoidance:** An approaching vehicle broadcasts its speed and GPS coordinates. Vehicles on intersecting streets detect the collision risk seconds before arriving at the blind intersection.
  * **Blind Spot Warnings:** Warns when passing or lane-changing while a vehicle occupies an unobservable blind angle.
  * **Cooperative Adaptive Cruise Control (CACC) / Platooning:** Fleets of trucks travel tightly packed at high speeds, coordinating acceleration and braking wirelessly within milliseconds to reduce aerodynamic drag and fuel consumption.

---

## Comparison: DSRC vs. Cellular V2X (C-V2X)
| Feature | DSRC (802.11p / WAVE) | Cellular V2X (C-V2X / 5G) |
| :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **Foundation** | IEEE 802.11 Wi-Fi adaptation | 3GPP Cellular Standards (Release 14–16+) |
| **Direct Mode** | Direct ad-hoc peer communication | Direct PC5 Sidelink interface |
| **Infrastructure Need** | Requires dedicated RSU deployment | Reuses existing cellular mobile towers |
| **Range & Latency** | Short range (<300m), low latency | Extended range, sub-millisecond in 5G URLLC |

---

## Key Exam Takeaways
* **OBU vs. RSU:** OBU is in-vehicle; RSU is stationary roadside infrastructure.
* **NLOS Advantage:** V2X radio signals detect hazards hidden behind buildings or blind corners where optical cameras and LiDAR fail.
* **Platooning Benefit:** Drastically reduces aerodynamic drag and fuel consumption via millisecond-coordinated automated braking.
* **C-V2X Standard:** Governed by 3GPP utilizing the PC5 direct sidelink interface.
